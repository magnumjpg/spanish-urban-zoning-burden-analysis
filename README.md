An advanced econometric and public choice research study quantifying the causal price distortions, supply inelasticity, and deadweight loss generated by municipal discretionary land-use planning (*Plan General de Ordenación Urbana - PGOU*) across **52 Spanish municipalities over a 15-year longitudinal panel (2009–2023, $N = 780$)**.

---

## Executive Summary

Across OECD economies, the housing affordability crisis is frequently misdiagnosed as pure financial speculation or market failure. In Spain, however, the root cause is fundamentally **institutional and regulatory**: under the state framework (*Texto Refundido de la Ley de Suelo y Rehabilitación Urbana*) and regional urbanism statutes, **all land is legally classified as non-buildable (*suelo no urbanizable*) by default**.

To build, land must be discretionarily reclassified (*recalificación*) by local municipal governments through bureaucratic General Urban Plans (*PGOU*) or ad-hoc municipal agreements (*convenios urbanísticos*). This legal architecture grants municipal councils an absolute political monopoly over the supply of building rights.

This research project designs, calibrates, and estimates an empirical econometric model comparing two institutional regimes:
1. **The Discretionary Municipal Monopoly (Status Quo PGOU):** Political rationing of building rights, 14–24 month licensing bottlenecks, and astronomical land reclassification markups (**24.2x** raw land value).
2. **The By-Right Liberalized Baseline (Counterfactual):** A property-rights baseline where all non-ecologically protected land is developable by default, licenses are issued via 2.5-month objective administrative compliance verification, and developers absorb demand shocks elastically.

### Key Empirical Findings

- **The Bureaucratic Delay Tax:** Under a Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE) panel specification with clustered standard errors, **each additional month of municipal licensing backlog increases housing prices by +1.53% ceteris paribus ($p < 0.001$)**. In major metros where licensing approval averages 18 to 22 months, administrative friction alone contributes **+22% to +26%** of total residential acquisition costs.
- **Artificial Land Rationing:** A 10 percentage point increase in the ratio of administratively frozen rustic land yields a **+2.69% price increase ($p = 0.0056$)**.
- **Supply Elasticity Collapse:** In Tier 1 Metros (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Málaga, Palma), supply elasticity under the discretionary PGOU regime is severely suppressed at **$\hat{\epsilon}_s^{PGOU} = 0.31$**. Under the By-Right counterfactual, elasticity expands by **3.8x to $\hat{\epsilon}_s^{ByRight} = 1.20$** (and up to **6.4x** in coastal dynamic hubs), dampening price spikes through volume expansion.
- **Total Social Welfare Loss (2009–2023):** Discretionary municipal zoning inflicted a cumulative economic burden of **€73.59 Billion** across the sample:
  - **€29.13 Billion** in Harberger Deadweight Loss (trades destroyed by artificial quotas).
  - **€44.46 Billion** in Tullock Rent Dissipation (wealth extracted from citizens into speculative land-banking, lobbying, and carrying costs).
  - An average burden of **€4,381.95 per resident**.

## Econometric Methodology & Identification Strategy

### 1. Panel Fixed Effects Model of Housing Prices

$$\ln(P_{it}) = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \beta_1 \text{Delay}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{Frozen}_{it} + \beta_3 \text{Premium}_{it} + \beta_4 \ln(\text{Corruption}_{it}) + \beta_5 \ln(\text{Income}_{it}) + \beta_6 \text{PopGrowth}_{it} + u_{it}$$

- $\alpha_i$: Municipality Fixed Effects (controls for time-invariant unobserved geographic amenities, topography, and municipal characteristics).
- $\lambda_t$: Year Fixed Effects (non-parametrically absorbs common macroeconomic shocks, ECB monetary policy, Euribor fluctuations, and nationwide inflation).
- Standard errors are clustered at the municipality level ($\text{Cov}_{\text{cluster}}$) to ensure robust inference against arbitrary serial correlation.

## Economic Welfare & Public Choice Metrics

### Harberger Triangle (Deadweight Loss)
$$DWL = \frac{1}{2} (P_{PGOU} - P_{ByRight}) \times (Q_{ByRight} - Q_{PGOU}) \times \overline{\text{DwellingSize}}$$
- **Cumulative DWL (2009–2023):** **€29.13 Billion**
- **Annual Average:** **€1.94 Billion / year**

### Tullock Rent Dissipation
$$\text{Rent Transfer} = (P_{PGOU} - P_{ByRight}) \times Q_{PGOU} \times \overline{\text{DwellingSize}}$$
- **Cumulative Wealth Transfer:** **€44.46 Billion**
- **Annual Average:** **€2.96 Billion / year**
- **Per Capita Cumulative Burden:** **€4,381.95 per resident** across the panel.
